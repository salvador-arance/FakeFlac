id: RF-20
title: For a lossless file with lossy evidence, estimate the source bitrate it was made from
priority: medium
milestone: H3
status: draft
---

When a lossless file (WAV, FLAC, ALAC, and the lossless formats of RF-19) is `Fake` because of lossy evidence, FakeFlac estimates the bitrate of the lossy source it was made from, so the user knows what quality the file really holds. Today the real bitrate cell of a lossless track is always `—` (RF-15 covers lossy files only; `decode-lossy.feature`).

Measurable acceptance (to be fixed at G1 of its spec):
- **Estimate.** From the measured cutoff and the existing `[lossy.cutoffs]` tables, with the rule that rules L3 to L5 already use (the nearest table row). Shown as an approximation (`≈128 kbps`) in the table, the detail panel and the CSV; `—` stays for `Real` lossless tracks.
- **Accuracy.** Measured with `just accept` on the calibration transcodes: the share of transcodes estimated within one table row of their setting, with a target fixed at G1.
- **Unchanged verdicts.** No verdict, cutoff, reason or evidence changes; only the estimate is added.

Source: owner's decision of 2026-11-20 (H3 extension, #184). AudioAuditor 2.0.0 shows an "Actual BR" for every format, but for lossless files it is either the file's size over its duration or a step lookup in an MP3-shaped table (`AudioAnalyzer.Quality.cs`, `EstimateBitrateFromCutoff`). Issue #201.

Spec: pending. Open questions in #201: which table when the source codec is unknown (MP3 and AAC differ at the same bitrate), and whether `Suspicious` tracks with a cutoff reason get an estimate too. The two `decode-lossy.feature` scenarios that require `—` for lossless rows change with this spec.
